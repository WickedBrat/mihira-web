=== Garuda Purana (Saroddhara) — Chapter V: The Signs of Sins ===
Sanskrit source: not yet sourced (GRETIL lookup still todo — see sources.json).
English source: Ernest Wood & S. V. Subrahmanyam, 1911 (Panini Office, Allahabad). Public domain — published before 1923; provided by user from sacred-texts.com download.
Verse numbering: native to this translation, 1-57 (combined labels like 'N-M' indicate the translators grouped consecutive Sanskrit verses into one rendered passage).

[5.1]
ENGLISH: Garuda said: Tell me, O Kes'ava, by what sins particular signs are produced, and to what sorts of birth such sins lead?

[5.2]
ENGLISH: The Blessed Lord said: The sins on account of which the sinful returning from hell come to particular births, and the signs produced by particular sins,--these hear from me.

[5.3]
ENGLISH: The murderer of a Brahman becomes consumptive, the killer of a cow becomes hump-backed and imbecile, the murderer of a virgin becomes leprous,--all three born as outcastes.

[5.4]
ENGLISH: The slayer of a woman and the destroyer of embryos becomes a savage full of diseases; who commits illicit intercourse, a eunuch; who goes with his teacher's wife, diseased-skinned.

[5.5]
ENGLISH: The eater of flesh becomes very red; the drinker of intoxicants, one with discoloured teeth; the Brahman who, on account of greed, eats what should not be eaten, becomes big-bellied.

[5.6]
ENGLISH: He who eats sweet foods, without giving to others, becomes swollen-necked; who gives impure food at a S'raddha ceremony is born a spotted leper.

[5.7]
ENGLISH: The man who, through pride, insults his teacher, becomes an epileptic; who despises the Vedas and the S'astras certainly becomes jaundiced.

[5.8]
ENGLISH: Who bears false witness becomes dumb; who breaks the meal-row [note: This refers to the custom among Brahmins of sitting in a row at meals, and rising together. Whoever gives different food to one than to another is said to break the row also.] becomes one-eyed; who interferes with marriage becomes lipless; who steals a book-is born blind.

[5.9]
ENGLISH: Who strikes a cow or a Brahman with his foot is born lame and deformed; who speaks lies becomes a stammerer, and who listens to them becomes deaf.

[5.10]
ENGLISH: A poisoner becomes insane; an incendiary becomes bald; who sells flesh becomes unlucky; who eats fled of other beings becomes diseased.

[5.11]
ENGLISH: Who steals jewels is born in a low caste; who steals gold gets diseased nails; who steals any metal becomes poverty-stricken.

[5.12]
ENGLISH: Who steals food becomes a rat; who steals grain becomes a locust; who steals water becomes a Chataka-bird [note: A bird fabled to live only on rain-drops.]; and who steals poison, a scorpion.

[5.13]
ENGLISH: Who steals vegetables and leaves becomes a peacock; perfumes, a musk-rat; honey, a gad-fly; flesh, a vulture; and salt, an ant.

[5.14]
ENGLISH: Who steals betel, fruits and flowers becomes a forest-monkey; who steal shoes, grass and cotton are born from sheeps' wombs.

[5.15]
ENGLISH: Who lives by violence, who robs caravans on the road, and who is fond of hunting, certainly becomes a goat in a butcher's house.

[5.16]
ENGLISH: Who dies by drinking poison becomes a black serpent on a mountain; whose feature is unrestrained becomes an elephant in a desolate forest.

[5.17]
ENGLISH: Those twice-born who do not make offering to the World-deities, and who eat all foods without consideration, become tigers in a desolate forest.

[5.18]
ENGLISH: The Brahmin who does not recite the Gayatri [note: A sacred mantra repeated every day by the twice-born caste.], who does not meditate at twilight, who is inwardly wicked while outwardly pious, becomes a crane.

[5.19]
ENGLISH: The Brahmin who officiates for one unfit to perform sacrifice becomes a village hog, and by too many sacrifices he becomes an ass; by eating without grace, a crow.

[5.20]
ENGLISH: The twice-born who does not impart learning to the deserving becomes a bull; the pupil who does not serve his teacher becomes an animal,--an ass or a cow.

[5.21]
ENGLISH: Who threatens and spits at his teacher, or browbeats a Brahman, is born as a Brahmin-fiend in a waterless wilderness.

[5.22]
ENGLISH: Who does not give to a twice-born according to his promise becomes a jackal; who is not hospitable to the goody becomes a howling Fire-face. [note: A class of evil spirits.]

[5.23]
ENGLISH: Who deceives a friend becomes a mountain-vulture; who cheats in selling, an owl; who speaks ill of caste and order is born a pigeon in a wood.

[5.24]
ENGLISH: Who destroys hopes and who destroys affection, who through dislike abandons his wife, becomes a ruddy goose for a long time.

[5.25]
ENGLISH: Who hates mother, father and teacher, who quarrels with sister and brother, is destroyed when an embryo in the womb, even for a thousand births.

[5.26]
ENGLISH: The woman who abuses her mother-in-law and father-in-law, and causes constant quarrels; becomes a leech; and she who scolds her husband becomes a louse.

[5.27]
ENGLISH: Who, abandoning her own husband, runs after another man, becomes a flying-fox, a house-lizard, or a kind of female serpent.

[5.28]
ENGLISH: He who cuts off his lineage, by embracing a woman of his own family, having become a hyena and a porcupine, is born from the womb of a bear.

[5.29]
ENGLISH: The lustful man who goes with a female ascetic becomes a desert fiend; who consorts with an immature girl becomes a huge snake in a wood.

[5.30]
ENGLISH: Who covets his teacher's wife, becomes a chameleon; who goes with the king's wife becomes corrupt; and with his friend's wife, a donkey.

[5.31]
ENGLISH: Who commits unnatural vice becomes a village pig; who consorts with a S'udra woman becomes bull; who is passionate becomes a lustful horse.

[5.32-33]
ENGLISH: Who feeds upon the eleventh-day offerings to the dead is born a dog. The devalaka is born from the womb of a hen. The wretch among twice-born who worships the deities for the sake of wealth is called a devalaka and is unfit to offer oblations to ale deities and forefathers.

[5.34]
ENGLISH: Those who are very sinful, having passed through dreadful hells produced by their great sins, are born here upon the exhaustion of their karma.

[5.35]
ENGLISH: The murderer of a Brahman goes into the womb of an ass, a camel and a she-buffalo; a drinker of intoxicants enters the wombs of a wolf, a dog and a jackal.

[5.36]
ENGLISH: The stealer of gold attains the condition of a worm, an insect and a bird. Who goes with his teacher's wife, goes to the condition of grass, bushes and plants.

[5.37]
ENGLISH: Who steals another's wife, who misappropriates deposits, who robs a Brahmin, is born as a Brahmin-fiend.

[5.38-40]
ENGLISH: The Brahmin's possessions acquired by deception, enjoyed even in friendship, afflict the family even for seven generations,--and by forcible robbery even as long as the moon and stars exist: A man may digest even iron filings, powdered stone, and poison; but where is the person in the three worlds who can digest a Brahmin's wealth! Chariots and troops supported by the wealth of a Brahmin crumble away in battle like artificial river-banks of sand.

[5.41-43]
ENGLISH: By appropriating temple property, by taking a Brahmin's possessions, and by neglecting Brahmins, families become broken up. He is called a neglector who, instead of making a gift to one who is well-read in the Vedas and S'astras and has resorted to him, gives it to some other, But it is no neglect if the Brahmin is without Veda-knowledge; it would be like offering to ashes instead of to the blazing fire near by.

[5.44]
ENGLISH: Having neglected, O Tarksya, and having experienced the results in the successive hells, he is born blind and in poverty, becoming not a giver but a beggar.

[5.45]
ENGLISH: Who takes away a plot of land, which was given by himself for another, is born for sixty thousand years as a worm in excrement.

[5.46]
ENGLISH: The sinner who takes back by force what has been given by himself, goes into hell until the coming of the deluge.

[5.47]
ENGLISH: Having given the means of subsistence and a piece of land, he should then protect it firmly. Who does not protect, but robs, is born as a lame dog.

[5.48]
ENGLISH: Who gives the means of support to Brahmins obtains fruit equal to that of a lakh of cows; who robs Brahmins of their means of sup port becomes an ape, a dog and a monkey.

[5.49]
ENGLISH: These and other signs and births, O Lord of Birds, are seen to be the karma of the embodied, made by themselves in this world.

[5.50]
ENGLISH: Thus the makers of bad karma, having experienced the tortures mf hell, are born with the residues of their sins, in these stated forms.

[5.51]
ENGLISH: Then, obtaining for thousands of lives the bodies of animals, they suffer from carrying burdens and other miseries.

[5.52]
ENGLISH: Having experienced as a bird the misery of cold, rain and heat, he afterwards reaches the human state, when the good and evil are balanced.

[5.53]
ENGLISH: Man and woman having come together, he becomes an embryo in due course. Having suffered the miseries from conception onwards to death, he again dies.

[5.54]
ENGLISH: Birth and death are the lot of all embodied beings; thus turns the wheel in the four kingdoms of beings.

[5.55]
ENGLISH: As the wheel of time turns, so mortals revolve by my magic. They revolve at one time of earth, at another in hell, held fast by the noose of karma.

[5.56]
ENGLISH: He who does not mike gifts becomes poverty--stricken and through poverty he commits sin; by the force of sin he goes to hell, and is again born in poverty and again becomes sinful.

[5.57]
ENGLISH: Karma which has been made, whether good or evil, must inevitably be suffered. Karma not suffered does not fade away even in tens of millions of ages.

--- END CHAPTER V ---
